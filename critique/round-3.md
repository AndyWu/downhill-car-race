## 1. Scene & Environment (2-Mile Country Dirt Road) [ Sonnet 5.5 Medium]
- **Failure 1:** The berm now *contains* the car but turns into a trap. Steered drift runs went at most 3.16 m (s=600, steer +0.35), 2.22 m (s=1500) and 3.23 m (s=2200) beyond the road edge, and a 30 s no-input run went 2.28 m. That fixes R2's −26 m excursions. But in the 90 s ai=5 race the no-input player sat pinned at lateral −4.6/−4.7 from t=20 to t=90, crawling at 1.1–1.2 m/s: 85 s off-road and 67 s below 2 m/s, covering s=104→191. `q-low.jpg` shows the truck climbing the grassy bank. A ditch that holds the car without guiding it back down is worse than the TLOU "the world pushes back" standard; it reads as glue.
- **Failure 2:** Puddles and dressing are fixed but the geometry budget is heavy. The `?scene=puddle` puddle is now a soft-edged irregular pool (`hazard-puddle.jpg`), and the s≈150 rectangle is gone: the hazards near s=150 are `mud@114 r8` and `rutpool@190 r1.2`, and `puddle-s150.jpg` shows no hard-edged plane. Dressing is now confined to the start and finish (`hazard-mud.jpg` has no bunting; R2 did). Scene cost with `renderer.info` at q=default, ai=5: **223 calls / 366,871 triangles at s=0**, 103/186,606 at s=600, 95/168,732 at s=1500, and 78/189,500 at s=3150. The start line alone is 367k triangles for hay bales, bunting and low-poly crowds, and in SwiftShader q=default still runs at 0.6 FPS.
- **Concrete Fix:** In `src/scene/ground.js`, give the berm an inner face slope of 18° (not near-vertical) and add a lateral restoring term in `surfaceAt` for |lateral| > hw. Feed `bump`/`drag` that fall off with `normalAt` so gravity rolls the car back toward the crown. Add a physics regression in `src/scene/sanity.mjs`: a no-input car must regain |lateral| < hw within 6 s at ≥ 3 m/s. In `src/scene/scenery.js`, merge the start-zone hay bales, bunting flags and crowd into ≤ 3 `InstancedMesh` and drop bale segments from 32 to 12. Target s=0 at ≤ 120 calls / 200k tris.

## 2. Network, Offline PWA & QR-WebRTC Star Topology [Opus 5.5 Medium]
- **Failure 1:** Uplink is now bigger than downlink. In the real game (`tests/critic-r3-net.mjs`: host + client, 10.01 s) the host sent 300 snapshots at 30.0 Hz, **98.2 B avg = 16.4 B/car**, 2,943 B/s per client (down from R2's 26 B/car). The *client* sent 74,604 B in the same window, about 7.5 KB/s, roughly 2.5× the host's per-client downlink, for one player's inputs. Redundant input repetition at 60 Hz is the likely cause. On a hotspot with 5 clients, uplink contention (≈37 KB/s of inputs into the host) now dominates.
- **Failure 2:** The lossy-link work is solid on paper and only partly real-world. Loopback at 5 % and 15 % loss with 20–80 ms jitter: interpolation error max 5.5/5.6 cm, ack p95 164/180 ms, max visual pop 3.62/2.60 cm per frame, 12.33 KiB/s for 5 clients, all ok. `net-offline.mjs` proves offlineReady waits for `activated` (log `installing→installed→activating→activated→<resolved>`). However, that test runs a 7-file / 77 KiB *stub* page, not the game's precache, and the lossy stage runs only in the harness (`net-fake-world.js`), never in the real two-page game path above.
- **Concrete Fix:** In `src/net/sync.js`, pack inputs as a 4-byte bitfield (steer int8, paddle impulses 2×uint4, flags), send them at 30 Hz with the last 2 frames redundantly, and drop repeats whose payload is unchanged. Target client tx ≤ 1.5 KB/s. Extend `tests/net-race.mjs` (Network-owned) to wrap the real `RTCDataChannel.send` with the same 15 % loss/jitter shim and assert a host/client position delta < 0.5 m. Run `net-offline.mjs` against the real `dist/` manifest.

## 3. Core Gameplay, Controls & Flag Physics [Sonnet 5.5 Medium]
- **Failure 1:** The headlight tap still works only some of the time. With `?debug=input` it toggled (`down headlight R 861 656 touch → up headlight held 6789 allowed 9916 → headlight -> true`), passing only because of the new stall tolerance, since SwiftShader made the tap "last" 6.8 s. In the controls-phase page, the identical tap at the same coordinates gave `headlightAfterTap:false`. That is 1/2 in R3 (0/3 in R1–R2). The gear drag works (`reverse:true`), and the handbrake holds 3.07 m/s² (11.92→5.77 m/s in 2 s).
- **Failure 2:** Paddle determinism is fixed but the player can be teleported backwards. `advance()` paddle runs from v=2 are now bit-identical (padA = padB = 107.92 m in 10 s vs coast 30.75/35.99 m, so paddling is about 3×). But in the controls page (ai=0) a 10 s paddle run returned **Δs = −64.7 m**: the player went *backwards* along the track, apparently because the new AI-style rescue/respawn acted on the player. Coast runs on the ai=5 page also differ (30.75 vs 35.99), because the teleported player collides with AI traffic. Pre-GO tilt calibration now works: ±28.6° reads ±28.6° and steer ±0.61, symmetric (R2 read +6.4/−50.9).
- **Concrete Fix:** In `src/main.js`, restrict the rescue/respawn path (from `src/ai/driver.js` integration) to `v.kind === 'ai'`. For the player, offer a manual "reset to road" on a long press of the gear lever instead. In `src/core/input.js` headlight `up()`, drop the role check on `pointerup` and use zone-of-release with a 600 ms `e.timeStamp` window plus stall credit. Add a `tests/core-r3.mjs` (Core-owned) case that taps twice and asserts on→off.

## 4. Audio & Diegetic Cockpit UI [Sonnet 5.5 Medium]
- **Failure 1:** The results card is honest now but lists the wrong order. With the player finishing at 1:35.27 (`finish.jpg`), the card reads "still rolling… ~142 s Buster, ~177 s Maeve, ~251 s Ace, ~266 s Barnaby, ~276 s Clementine". That fixes R2's instant DNF, but it ranks the tier-5 "Pro" Ace 3rd of 5 AIs and tier-2 Clementine *behind* tier-1 Barnaby. The diegetic payoff tells the player the AI tiers are meaningless (see §7).
- **Failure 2:** Glyphs now behave (opacity 0.32 at t=0, then 0/0/0.01/0 … 0 through 60 s; R2 flashed 0.26 at 50 s and 60 s, `glyphs-60s.jpg`). The audio bank has measurable, distinct spectra (`tests/audio-metrics.mjs`): mud has 80 % energy < 250 Hz (centroid 588 Hz), gravel peaks at 2–4 kHz (centroid 2,836 Hz), and rock has crest 12.0. But *sand* has the brightest centroid of all (4,137 Hz, 18 % > 8 kHz), brighter than gravel, which is physically backwards: sand should be a soft low-mid hiss. Ambient is only −35.5 dBFS, 10 dB under the surfaces, so there is no rural bed (wind, birds, distant tractor) to ground the scene.
- **Concrete Fix:** In `src/audio/bank.js`, run the sand clip through a one-pole low-pass at 2.5 kHz with a 300–900 Hz bandpass body (target centroid 1.2–1.8 kHz), and raise ambient to −26 dBFS with a 3-layer bed (wind noise LP 600 Hz, sparse bird chirps, low tractor drone at 0.2 gain). In `src/ui/index.js`, sort "still rolling" rows by projected time, which it already does, and fix the projection to use current speed and remaining distance with a slope term once §7 is fixed.

## 5. The 10 Homemade Soap Box Trucks [Sonnet 5.5 Medium]
- **Failure 1:** Mud on the cockpit is close to invisible. After 3 s of rendered driving through mud (`mud-splatter-rendered.jpg`, `mud-splatter-rendered-2.jpg`, 90 + 40 frames at 1/30 s, each rendered), the paddles, lever knobs and the wheel rim look the same as in the clean `cockpit-day-start.jpg`. Only a few faint brown smudges show on the steering-wheel spokes. A TLOU-grade pass would leave the paddle faces visibly caked and the knobs dulled.
- **Failure 2:** Race numbers are now unique (42, 5, 13, 21, 10 and the rest, `trucks-contact-sheet.jpg`; R2 had two 13s), and the rears are distinct at 25 m (truck 0 spare tyre, truck 2 orange crates, truck 3 a "5" barrel, truck 7 teal tail-plane, truck 8 a ladder frame with a flag). The steering wheel is smaller, and the knobs are now turned wood (`hazard-gravel.jpg`). But the paddle boards still have a cartoon-saturated yellow-ochre albedo. Next to the desaturated, aged fence and barn wood they look like freshly painted toys, not the scavenged planks the truck descriptions promise.
- **Concrete Fix:** In `src/vehicles/parts.js`/`kit.js`, have the `mudCoat` uniform drive an albedo multiply to `#3b2a1c` and roughness → 0.95 through a world-space noise mask biased to the lower 60 % of each part. It should reach 0.5 coverage after 1.5 s of mud contact (read `state.surface==='mud'` time from physics). Desaturate the paddle wood albedo by 35 % and add a grey weathering layer (`materials.woodWeathered` blend 0.6).

## 6. Graphics, Lighting & PBR Shaders [Sonnet 5.5 Medium]
- **Failure 1:** Lens water drops show up where there is no water. `cockpit-midtrack-speed.jpg` (teleport to s=1500 on dry dirt, 1.5 s) shows about 7 clear refracting droplets on the lens with no puddle or stream anywhere in the run. `puddle-s150.jpg` has drops *before* the car reaches the water. The new splatter itself is improved: when driving through mud at render time it is now 1–2 small streaked smudges in the lower half, with no halos (R2 had about 18 haloed discs in the sky). But it is so faint (`mud-splatter-rendered.jpg`, a single smear at about (280, 500)) that the effect is now near-invisible for mud while it misfires for dry dirt.
- **Failure 2:** Frame cost improved but tiers are still expensive. SwiftShader FPS was q-low 1.8, q-medium 1.4, q-default 0.6 and chase-ai 1.0 (R2: 1.0/0.4/0.2/1.4), a real ≈2–3× gain for low and medium. q-default remains 3× q-medium, with the start-line 367k triangles (see §1). Gravel uses hex tiling now (`hazard-gravel.jpg`), with no visible grid repeat, but it still reads as dense cobble paving and not loose 1–3 cm farm gravel over dirt. The chase cam regressed: `chase-ai-race.jpg` (6 s) shows the camera parked at the start beside a hay bale while the pack is a dot about 150 m ahead (R2's chase view framed the cars).
- **Concrete Fix:** In `src/graphics/postfx.js`, have `addSplatter` water drops only on `state.surface in {'water','rutpool','puddle'}` contact edges (latched in physics), and clear the drop list on `teleport`. Make mud drops 2–4 per hit at alpha 0.85 with streak length ∝ speed. In `src/graphics/textures.js`, gravel: lower the stone coverage mask to 45 % and mix with `dirtRoad` albedo. In `src/core/camera.js` chase mode, follow `playerV.group` with a critically damped spring (ω=6) and never snap to the start-transform.

## 7. The 5 Tiered AI Agents [Sonnet 5.5 Medium]
- **Failure 1:** In-game tier inversion. In the 90 s ai=5 race, s at t=90 was Barnaby 852, Clementine 696, Buster 1186, Maeve 1124, **Ace 859**. The Pro is level with the Rookie, 327 m behind Buster, and the Cautious tier-2 is last. Ace dropped to 4.9 m/s at t=20 and 1.4 m/s at t=90, with 13 s off-road. Buster took the lead at t≈20 and held it, with only **1 AI lead change** in 90 s. Stalls are rarer (slow-seconds: Barnaby 1, Clementine 4, Ace 1), but the order is wrong.
- **Failure 2:** The headless sim disagrees with the game and fails its own gate. `node tests/ai-sim.mjs 3` (real physics) averages Ace 262.4 < Maeve 291.5 < Buster 332.6 < Clementine 339.0 < Barnaby 367.8 s, but prints **FAIL: Barnaby/Ace ratio 1.40 > 1.35**. Buster and Clementine are effectively tied (seed 1: 331.2 vs 331.1, and in seed 3 Buster at 343.3 was slower than Clementine at 343.0). RESCUE and STALL events persist (Barnaby STALL6s@3126, Maeve RESCUE2 STALL4s@3142, Buster off-road 29.1 s in seed 1). The sim ranks Ace first while the game puts him 4th, so the sim is not modelling the in-game start (grid slots, ramp, car-to-car contact).
- **Concrete Fix:** In `tests/ai-sim.mjs` (AI-owned), spawn all five on the real `startTransform(slot)` grid with car-to-car collisions on, and assert the in-game order at t=90. In `src/ai/driver.js`, have Ace/Maeve apply `carAvoid` lateral offsets at ≥ 6 m look-ahead instead of braking behind traffic, and make the stuck-recovery fire only when no car is within 4 m (Ace's t=20 slowdown is a blocked-behind-traffic brake). In `src/ai/profiles.js`, raise Barnaby `paddle.cadence` to 0.9× of base to get the ratio under 1.35, and give Buster `speed.cap` 17 vs Clementine 15 to separate tiers 2 and 3 by ≥ 15 s.
